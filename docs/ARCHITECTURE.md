# Implementation notes

Glissform is a macOS app for subtle animations and quality-of-life improvements across everyday interactions. The current implementation contains Infinite Screen plus shared settings, appearance, permission presentation, and menu infrastructure. The lid sensor, capture pipeline, and projection described below belong to Infinite Screen; they are not requirements for every future experience. Additional experiences have not been implemented yet.

- **LidSensor.swift:** read-only IOKit HID access, Apple vendor/product `05ac:8104`, usage `0020:008a`, feature report 1. A serial worker requests whole-degree readings at 30 Hz while idle and 60 Hz during active gestures, with bounded reconnect backoff. Each reading carries its monotonic host acquisition time, recorded after the HID read and before main-queue delivery; the report contains no hardware timestamp. Polling frequency does not establish the sensor's actual update frequency or improve its angle resolution. Protocol support is undocumented.
- **ClosingMotion.swift:** gesture and threshold state, including awake reversal and startup/reset behavior. Optional pause restoration uses monotonic sensor timestamps, a one-degree min/max stability band, and a 0.5–10 second wait supported internally for tests; the app uses a fixed two seconds. Movement beyond that band resets the clock, as do invalid readings or gaps over 250 ms. Expiry deactivates the gesture and latches suppression until the lid is strictly above the trigger. Automatic starting-angle mode separately learns a 20–130° idle lid angle after two seconds of fresh readings inside a one-degree band; the manual trigger remains the fallback until then. A learned angle overrides that trigger without changing the active gesture plane and survives lifecycle reset for the current session. When pause restoration is enabled, its expiry can adopt the paused angle while keeping the desktop-restored latch until the lid opens above the new angle. Lifecycle reset clears the clocks and latch; low-angle startup remains unarmed until a valid rest is learned or the lid opens above the fallback.
- **DesktopCapture.swift:** one sRGB ScreenCaptureKit screenshot per gesture, with dimensions derived from the filter's content rectangle and point-to-pixel scale, checked against the overlay drawable and returned buffer. The identified overlay window is excluded. If the hidden overlay is missing from metadata, application exclusion with explicit exceptions for other known Glissform windows prevents recursive capture while preserving settings where metadata permits. Unknown or foreign overlay ownership fails closed. A shared, generation-protected metadata cache has a 30-second lifetime; connection prewarming and a subsequent gesture can share discovery without capturing pixels. Settings presentation, activation and close invalidate the metadata, as do display/session/access changes; generation checks reject late discovery and capture results. No stream is started. Pixels remain in memory.
- **WakeOpening.swift:** numeric eligibility for reusing the closing image after sleep; it owns no pixels. The coordinator requires a completed closing screenshot and a recent 0–20° reading before arming an attempt. Duplicate wake notifications do not reset its three-second deadline. Fresh rising readings spanning at least two degrees may prepare the retained frame while more than three degrees remain below the reference. Samples more than 250 ms apart restart movement evidence before preparation and cancel pending preparation. Reversal by more than two degrees, expiry, unavailable desktop, or reaching the reference cancels a pending reveal. Once visible, ordinary reversal and pause behavior take over.
- **DesktopAvailability.swift:** preflight permission, active/awake display, console/logged-in session, frontmost application, and the optional undocumented `CGSSessionScreenIsLocked` hint. Missing session/frontmost evidence fails closed; the lock field itself is normally absent on an unlocked desktop, so its absence alone is not proof of unlock. The coordinator also listens for undocumented distributed `com.apple.screenIsLocked` / `com.apple.screenIsUnlocked` notifications and public session-switch notifications. The coordinator keeps a sleep-associated closing snapshot hidden through lock, but session deactivation clears it. This is a prototype heuristic requiring physical OS-version validation, not a public unlock guarantee or security boundary. `canBecomeVisibleWithoutLogin` remains false; no private window spaces or login helpers are used.
- **ScreenProjection.swift / EffectRenderer.swift:** runtime-compiled Metal projection and frost/shadow, with a fixed viewpoint approximation. Camera distance is four screen heights and eye height 1.1 screen heights, relative to the reference image plane. Every physical degree maps to one rendered degree, with no 89° fold clamp. `MPSImageGaussianPyramid` prepares blur levels once per screenshot; the pyramid is reused through closing and eligible wake opening. Its kernels are warmed with synthetic pixels during renderer setup. sRGB textures decode for linear-light filtering and shading, then encode into the sRGB output. Frost strength grows with displayed progress through `(1 - exp(-3p)) / (1 - exp(-3))`; a smooth vertical gradient scales the radius parameter from 1.5% of image height at the hinge to 9% at the top. A normalized 3×3 reconstruction kernel samples a finer pyramid level in nine taps to avoid magnifying coarse mip cells into blocky colour patches. The level follows `0.5 * log2(1 + 0.5 * radius²)`; both the level and sample offsets approach the untouched base image at zero. This is visual tuning, not a reconstruction of the supplied reference shader. Broad shadow strength follows `p^2.2` with the full-height gradient and final top 5% black plateau. A separate contact shadow is anchored to the physical top edge: at displayed closure angle `d` in degrees, its strength is `0.88 * (1 - exp(-d / 3))`, its depth is `0.035 + 0.20 * (1 - exp(-d / 22))` screen heights, and its falloff is `exp(-(y / depth)^1.6)`. The two shadows multiply transmittance. This establishes bezel depth within the first few degrees without darkening the whole screen early. Fixed 8×8 spatial dithering distributes final 8-bit rounding without temporal noise; the identity frame and exact black bypass it. The shader emits opacity premultiplied in encoded sRGB for Core Animation composition through a nonopaque Metal layer, while retaining linear-light filtering and shading.
- **MotionSmoothing.swift:** acquisition-timed, exact critically damped filtering for quantized angles. Ingestion advances the trajectory at each sensor timestamp; presentation-time queries evaluate it without changing sensor cadence or history. Velocity is retained when response time changes; reversals discard old-direction momentum. Averaged per-degree cadence selects the response smoothly. The usual 35 ms time constant shortens above 30 degrees/second and grows for sparse readings up to 100 ms; reversals and large discontinuities use 12.5 ms. Boundary transitions use bounded quintic curves with a compatible incoming velocity, exact endpoints, and no fixed-target overshoot. Smoothing deliberately trades some tracking delay for steadier movement. Evaluating a future presentation timestamp settles toward the latest reported target; it does not extrapolate new lid movement.
- **main.swift:** menu controls, settings coordination, snapshot preparation/reveal, overlay ordering and click blocking, sensor/capture coordination, and cleanup across sleep, display changes, cancellation, and quit.
- **OverlayWindow.swift:** a borderless, non-activating `NSPanel` that joins all Spaces and other applications, including native full-screen Spaces. It does not become key or main and does not hide when Glissform deactivates. This presentation is independent of the app's regular/accessory activation policy, so opening settings does not strand the effect on a desktop Space. Runtime and synthetic lifecycle checks share the same window setup.
- **SettingsWindow.swift:** native SwiftUI settings interface hosted in an AppKit window, including live sensor and permission state, persisted animation controls, and concise privacy information.

The first closing screenshot frame is prepared at identity while the window is hidden, then the window is revealed at full opacity and geometry begins a 100 ms handoff. This removes the old live/frozen-image entrance dissolve. Wake opening starts from the current fold and fades in over 50 ms. Ordinary returns take 60–180 ms according to remaining distance and rendered velocity; pause restorations take 100–300 ms according to deformation. Geometry and material reach identity before a 50 ms fade exposes the live desktop. Reclosing starts from the current rendered position and compatible velocity and cancels the old finish.

`CAMetalDisplayLink` owns production rendering and supplies target presentation timestamps. It requests the screen's maximum refresh rate and one frame of latency; these are scheduling preferences, not guaranteed pacing. MetalKit's internal draw loop remains paused. The display link pauses at rest. Hidden first-frame preparation detaches it before acquiring a drawable; production display callbacks use only their supplied drawable. The synthetic lifecycle harness can explicitly render offscreen without competing for a display-linked drawable. Motion and shader opacity share this clock instead of separate window-alpha timers. A transparent final frame permits cleanup after GPU completion; presentation callbacks supply diagnostics and are not an unbounded prerequisite for releasing mouse access. A main-run-loop watchdog scheduled for 800 ms ends a stuck return if rendering callbacks stop. Sleep, lock, display changes, revoked access, and quit still hide immediately. Snapshot tokens and renderer transition generations reject stale asynchronous work. The capture-source interface permits lifecycle checks to resolve synthetic screenshots after cancellation without requesting screen access.

Wake opening reuses the completed closing image and prepares its first frame at the current fold, bypassing the closing entrance handoff. `EffectRenderer.pauseSnapshot()` stops drawing and invalidates transition callbacks while retaining the same pixel buffer, CVMetalTexture wrapper, and filtered texture/mipmaps. `prepareRetainedSnapshot()` renders from those resources without invoking ScreenCaptureKit or uploading/regenerating the image. `clear()` still releases them. The coordinator separates a retained hidden image from an active snapshot; only a completed, active closing gesture with recent near-closed evidence may cross sleep. A pending/failed capture, a restored desktop after a pause, or another sleep during an opening leaves no cached wake attempt. There is no fresh-capture fallback.

Eligibility, desktop availability, permission, and sensor freshness are checked before and after GPU preparation. A 50 ms timer bounds waiting until reveal/cancellation or three seconds after the first wake. Sensor restart and display reconnection begin immediately. Display identity, frame, scale, and connected screen layout must match; real reconfiguration clears the cache, while redundant unchanged display notifications preserve it. The overlay is transparent, nonblocking, and paused during retention. Gesture completion, cancelled/skipped wake, session switch, display change, revoked access, disable/threshold changes, and quit release all image resources. Screen-access checks use the existing grant; no screenshot stream, new permission, disk image cache, or sleep assertion is created. Local unified wake logs report retention eligibility, wake, first sensor delivery, cached-frame request/preparation, reveal requests, and skips, without desktop content. Submission/readiness timestamps are not physical presentation timestamps. Opt-in `MotionTiming` and `AnimationTiming` debug diagnostics report numeric HID read counts/cost, capture metadata/pixel/preparation timings and dimensions, and drawable presentation intervals/input age; they contain no desktop pixels, titles, or captured application identities. See [TESTING.md](TESTING.md) for invocation and interpretation.

Infinite Screen uses a frozen image viewed through a simulated moving panel. It does not track the viewer's head, intercept the lock screen, or keep the system awake.

Ordinary lock hints during sleep or before wake preparation preserve the hidden frame and original deadline. If lock arrives before actual sleep during a completed closing gesture at 0–20°, a one-second grace period holds the image; repeated lock hints do not extend it, and unlock without sleep or grace expiry releases it. Public session-deactivation events always clear the image. After wake, sensor samples are evaluated while locked: reaching the reference or expiry clears the cache, with no capture or reveal. Unlock does not reset the deadline or start preparation by itself. A lock during preparation or visible opening cancels that attempt completely, including asynchronous results.

Sensor health compares both the last actual reading and the most recent restart against its one-second limit. A heartbeat queued during sleep cannot treat the old timestamp as a failed restart at 0–4 ms after wake. Duplicate wake notifications do not move that restart time. This startup allowance does not mark any reading fresh: retained-frame preparation still requires real sensor samples and retains the existing 250 ms freshness and three-second wake bounds.

The settings window controller switches the app to regular activation when presenting settings and to accessory activation when that window closes. The Dock entry therefore follows settings, independently of the overlay window. Closing the last window does not terminate the app; sensor, capture, menu bar, and interruption handling continue. Menu bar Settings and application reopen events present the retained window again. This uses ordinary AppKit activation policies and needs no extra permission or login helper.

The settings window opens directly on the experience page, with native controls and semantic system colors. The footer opens Privacy & About; a Back button (⌘[) returns to the experience. There is no shared logo header or segmented navigation. An original Canvas illustration projects synthetic screen contours and a MacBook lid in perspective; the illustration follows the live reading, but does not reproduce the Metal effect pixel for pixel. SettingsModel synchronizes manual and automatic trigger controls, the learned angle, enable state, live angle, permission, and runtime status. The preview card groups the illustration and threshold controls, separated by a divider. Separate automatic-angle and pause-to-resume settings cards follow below it; runtime readiness remains in the menu bar. A shared screen-access card appears above the preview only while access is missing or during a three-second confirmation after a detected grant. It remains available in Privacy & About for status and management. Authorized launches omit the main-page card; revocation restores it. The main view owns the cancellable confirmation task. The shared root measures each page’s natural height and resizes the window while retaining its top edge and 32-point bottom padding. A shared scroll view handles content above the available-screen height cap. The current angle remains above the illustration; the slider has no live-angle marker. Preview and pause settings cards share a background, corner radius, and border, with a 16-point gap. The privacy footer has reserved bottom padding. Privacy & About, also opened by the standard About Glissform menu command, shows the app name and version, the privacy-information card, and the separate access card. There is no embedded access section or divider in the privacy card and no description or text below the cards. Hardware and alpha limitations remain in README and testing documentation.

The in-window preview has local observable state and a finite SwiftUI task. It snapshots the displayed lid angle and reference plane when playback starts, then immediately begins a synthetic close/reopen cycle back to that angle, without an opening hold. Its closing target is 22° or the starting angle if already lower, so starting the preview never first opens a nearly closed illustration. It stops on request or navigation away, and never calls DesktopCapture or the overlay renderer. Reduce Motion replaces playback with a still preview and disables interpolation of the live illustration. All content surfaces are opaque and adapt to system appearance; controls use the system accent. These accessibility changes apply to the settings illustration, not to the actual desktop effect.

Settings buttons share a ButtonStyle for hover and pressed feedback; activation and cancellation remain owned by SwiftUI Button. The AppKit slider preserves system tracking and keyboard input with a solid-white thumb that changes size slightly on hover/press. The angle field includes a raised degree symbol and left-side arrow buttons, each with independent hover/pressed feedback and hold-to-repeat. A SwiftUI capsule switch keeps its thumb solid white, applies hover/press feedback to the track, matches the System Settings proportions, uses Button for activation, and exposes a native Toggle accessibility representation. The slider and switch add no outer hover surface or border; arrow highlights stay inside their own hit areas. The angle field adds an editing outline. The settings window ends field editing on an outside mouse click before forwarding that same event, so other controls still respond on the first click. Feedback overlays do not intercept input, disabled controls suppress feedback, and transitions respect Reduce Motion.

Pause restoration uses the shared return-to-flat and fade-out path with a deformation-dependent return duration and snapshot cancellation tokens. Only fresh sensor samples can trigger the two-second pause; the return cleanup watchdog operates after restoration has begun. The pause and automatic-angle toggles are stored in UserDefaults; the learned angle is session-only. Automatic selection places the threshold one degree below the rounded held angle. A restored gesture keeps the held angle as its reopening gate, so the lower threshold cannot rearm it during the same pause. Changing the pause toggle restarts tracking without clearing an already-restored gesture latch. Changing automatic selection cancels an active gesture and starts learning afresh. Editing the manual angle does not interrupt a gesture anchored to a learned angle. The app uses the motion model's fixed two-second default and no longer reads or writes a user-selected duration. The pause card is a single row with the shared solid-colour icon tile at 16% opacity. The menu status reports when the desktop has been restored.

SettingsDesign.swift defines the settings spacing, typography, radii, semantic surfaces, appearance-specific icon colours, and interaction timings. SettingsWindow.swift consumes those foundations and shares its card surface across the main page and About; the full-width divider is used within the Infinite Screen preview/control card. Native controls retain platform keyboard and accessibility behaviour; custom arrows use 24 × 20-point targets with a shared disabled opacity. See [DESIGN_SYSTEM.md](DESIGN_SYSTEM.md) for the component rules and deliberate optical exceptions.

IconlyIcon.swift supplies cached local SVG template images. Bold / Regular is the app default; original Outline and Bulk sets and the previous Custom mix remain available for developer comparison. Custom card variants alter only secondary opacity, not source paths. The `--dev` build flag enables the Developer menu and session-only style shortcuts; normal builds omit it. SwiftUI interface icons update without resetting feature state. `BrandIcon.swift` loads the cached 18-point `GlissformMenuBar` image from the main asset catalog. The build exports ClearDark directly from `Artwork/Glissform.icon` using Icon Composer at 1× and 2×. `scripts/make-menu-template.swift` converts luminance into alpha, preserving the original contours and wave as monochrome detail. The result is compiled as a template image set alongside the app icon. AppKit supplies the menu bar foreground colour; developer icon switching does not replace it. See [ICONLY.md](ICONLY.md) for provenance and [DESIGN_SYSTEM.md](DESIGN_SYSTEM.md) for current tokens.

The brand app icon is separate from Iconly. `Artwork/Glissform.icon` holds a shaded glass-wave PNG and native appearance definitions. Clear and tinted are derived from the same artwork. `scripts/build.sh` uses Xcode 26+ `actool` to compile the native catalog and legacy `.icns`, merges the generated icon metadata into the main bundle, updates the `.app` directory's modification date for Launch Services, and signs afterward. Presenting settings resets `NSApp.applicationIconImage` to `nil` after restoring regular activation; this restores the bundled icon rather than assigning a static bitmap, leaving macOS in control of system style selection. Privacy & About reads the application icon supplied by AppKit; this in-app image does not live-follow Finder's style changes.
